# CA HSR 2026 Business Plan — Capital Cost Summary

Source: *2026 Business Plan Technical Supporting Document: Capital Cost Basis of
Estimate Report*, California High-Speed Rail Authority, May 2026 (memo dated
5/15/2026). 154 pages. File in repo root:
`2026 Business Plan - Technical Suppoprting Document - 2026-Business-Plan-Basis-of-Estimate-A11Y.pdf`.

All dollars are **Year of Expenditure (YOE)** — inflated to the year each dollar
is expected to be spent — unless noted. `$M` = millions, `$B` = billions.

---

## How to read this document

### Four scenarios, nested — never add them together

The report prices four alternative buildouts. Each one contains the one before
it, so the totals overlap.

| Scenario | Short name | Route | Adds over the previous |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **M-B** (early operating segment) | Merced → Bakersfield | — |
| 2 | **SF-B** | San Francisco → Bakersfield | SF–San Jose, Gilroy–Wye |
| 3 | **SF-P** (interim stage) | San Francisco → Palmdale | Bakersfield–Palmdale |
| 4 | **Phase 1** | San Francisco → Los Angeles / Anaheim | Palmdale–Burbank, Burbank–LA, LA–Anaheim |

Phase 1 is the full plan. The SF-P interim stage is what Phase 1 looks like if
Bakersfield–Palmdale finishes before a route into the LA Basin.

**This is why the appendix has four sections.** Appendix C.1–C.4 is one
section per scenario, each repeating the segments it contains. Segments shared
between scenarios cost more in larger scenarios because they get more track,
platforms, and power (e.g. Bakersfield Extension: $3.5B in M-B, $6.6B in Phase 1).

### P65 vs P30

- **P65** — 65% probability the final cost stays at or below this number. Every
  headline total in the report is P65.
- **P30** — the tighter number the Authority will manage budgets to.
- The appendix line items are **P30**. The P65 − P30 gap is lumped into the
  program-wide **contingency** line (SCC 90.00). So appendix totals equal the
  P65 headline totals, but the individual line items are understated by that gap.

### Early tables vs appendix — which one you want

| You want to show… | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Headline cost per scenario, change since last plan, how much is already spent | **Tables 2–6** (pp. 3–7) | Only place with prior-estimate and spent-to-date columns |
| Cost by **geography** (which stretch of line costs what) | **Appendix C segment totals** (below) | Clean per-segment numbers that sum exactly to each scenario total |
| Cost by **what it buys** (tunnels, stations, land, trains, consultants) | **Appendix C full-scenario rollups** (C.1.6, C.2.8, C.3.12, C.4.9) | Standard FRA cost categories (SCC codes) |
| Money **not** in the totals | **Tables 7 / 30** | Shared-benefit costs, excluded from every scenario |
| Station-by-station or discipline detail | Chapter 4 tables (M-B only) | Only M-B gets this breakdown |

For a user-friendly breakdown, the appendix is the better base: its numbers are
complete and internally consistent. The early tables mix geographic and
accounting categories in the same column (e.g. "Merced to Bakersfield Balance",
"Program Wide Support, Trainsets, and Other Balance").

### Phase 1 scope was cut, not just re-priced

Phase 1 dropped from $127.9B (2024) to $126.1B (2026). Footnote 4 to Table 5
says the 2024 scope, re-estimated with the new bottom-up method, would now cost
**$231.3B**. Table 28 shows what was cut versus the 2024 plan:

| Element | 2024 BP | 2026 BP | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aerial guideway | 48 route mi | 31 | −17 |
| At-grade guideway | 213 route mi | 195 | −18 |
| Bored tunnels | 53 route mi | 23 | −30 |
| Track | 3,315,840 track ft | 2,883,427 | −432,413 (−82 mi) |
| HSR stations | 12 | 11 | −1 |
| Maintenance-of-way facilities | 3 | 2 | −1 |

The report describes the Bay Area and LA Basin approach as running on upgraded
existing lines (Caltrain, Union Pacific, Metrolink) "at a fraction of the cost
of a fully dedicated alignment". How much of the $105B gap that accounts for is
not broken out.

## Inconsistencies spotted

- **Bookend spending:** $854M spent in Table 3 (as of Feb 28, 2026) and Table 13
  (as of Dec 31, 2025), but $847M in Table 32 (as of Feb 28, 2026).
- **Prior-estimate column for the M-B row:** the "2025 SPUR" value for
  Merced–Bakersfield is $32,654M in Table 3, $29,552M in Table 4, and $31,692M
  in Table 6.
- **Table 7 vs Table 30:** Central Valley shared benefit is $410M in Table 7
  and $409M in Table 30; Palmdale–Burbank is $180M vs $179M. Likely rounding.
- **Table 3 total** prints as "35,6622". The trailing 2 is footnote 2, so the
  total is $35,662M.
